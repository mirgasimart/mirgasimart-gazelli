# Mirgasim Studio Manual: Material Sourcing & Execution Guide

This practical manual is prepared specifically for the painter **Mirgasim** to execute the 5 commissioned fine art canvases. It details the precise sourcing locations across Baku and the Absheron Peninsula, preparation formulas, archival binding chemistry, and step-by-step painterly application protocols.

---

## 1. Geological & Biological Material Input Sourcing Guide

All materials in the brief must be gathered from authentic regional sources across the Absheron Peninsula:

| Raw Material | Sourcing Location in Absheron / Baku | How to Obtain / Extraction Method | Studio Preparation Protocol |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Masazir Pink Lake Salt** | **Lake Masazir** (Masazır gölü, ~15 km NW of Baku) | Collect raw crust directly along the eastern or southern shoreline, or acquire unrefined industrial chunks from local salt workers. | Gently break into medium/coarse crystals (2–6 mm). Do NOT wash in water. Dehydrate in low oven (60°C) to remove moisture before sealing with acrylic binder. |
| **Absheron Limestone (Gülbax / Badamdar stone)** | **Garadagh or Shuvalan quarries**, or local stone-cutting yards (*daş bazarı*) | Collect stone offcuts, soft quarry rubble, and fine stone dust (*əhəng tozu*) from local stone carvers (*daş yonanlar*). | Sift limestone dust through fine mesh (100–200 µm) for primer ground; crush small chunks into pebble-sized porous fragments for 3D textural impasto. |
| **Coquina (Shell Limestone / Balıqqulağı daşı)** | **Turkan, Shuvalan, or Mardakan coastal quarries** | Acquire porous, shell-rich coquina slabs or demolition fragments rich in visible bivalve casts. | Saw into thin relief pieces (5–15 mm thick) or crush into coarse aggregate containing intact shell fragments. |
| **Absheron Coastal Beach Sand** | **Bilgah, Pirshaghi, or Turkan beaches** | Scoop coastal sand from the natural dune waterline where shell fragments naturally accumulate. | Rinse 3 times with freshwater to remove sea salinity; bake dry in sunlight or oven; sift to separate fine sand from crushed shell grit. |
| **Caspian Shells (*Didacna* bivalves)** | **Dubandi, Turkan, or Pirshaghi shorelines** | Walk the high-tide line to hand-collect empty, weathered *Didacna* cockle shells (*Didacna trigonoides*, *Didacna pyramidata*). | Soak in 5% hydrogen peroxide or diluted bleach for 2 hours to sterilize and whiten; rinse thoroughly and air-dry. |
| **Mud-Volcano Clay** | **Lokbatan Mud Volcano** (Lökbatan) or **Dashgil Mud Volcano** (Daşgil, near Gobustan) | Collect fresh extruded volcanic mud from active gryphons/salses in airtight buckets, plus naturally sun-dried craquelure chunks from the crater edges. | Sift wet mud to remove large stones; blend with acrylic binder or PVA to regulate natural drying craquelure fissures. |
| **Natural Bitumen / Asphaltite** | **Binagadi Bitumen Lake & Tar Pits** (Binaqədi), or **Kirmaki / Fatmai outcrops** | Collect natural surface asphaltite and semi-viscous bitumen seeping from natural fissures. | Dissolve gently in odorless mineral spirits or mix directly into heavy gloss acrylic gel/oil painting medium for rich velvety black glazes. |
| **Pyrite (Fool's Gold)** | **Mud-volcano mineral ejecta** (Gobustan / Lokbatan) or local mineral markets (Nizami mineral shops in Baku) | Collect metallic brassy sulfide nodule aggregates from mud volcano breccia deposits. | Crush gently into 1–4 mm metallic crystalline aggregates using a steel mortar and pestle. |

## 2. Archival Chemistry & Binding Recipes

To ensure the physical substances remain permanently bonded to the canvas without flaking, cracking, or degrading over decades:

### Recipe A: Absheron Mineral Gesso Ground (For All Canvases)
- **Ingredients**:
  - 40% Traditional Titanium White Acrylic Gesso
  - 30% Fine Sifted Absheron Limestone Powder (*əhəng tozu*)
  - 20% Absheron Coastal Beach Sand (pre-washed)
  - 10% Extra Heavy Acrylic Matte Gel (Golden or Liquitex)
- **Application**: Apply with wide palette knives and masonry trowels onto heavy-duty 450 gsm linen canvas. Scrape and texture while wet to create a porous, stone-like fresco support.

### Recipe B: Masazir Salt Crystal Encrustation (Archival Sealing)
- **Problem**: Raw lake salt can absorb ambient humidity (*deliquescence*).
- **Solution**:
  1. Embed dried Masazir salt crystals into wet **Heavy Acrylic Gloss Gel**.
  2. Once dried, spray with 2 coats of **Archival Matte/Satin Mineral Fixative** (e.g., Lascaux or Krylon UV Archival Varnish) to seal each crystal from moisture while preserving its natural facets.

### Recipe C: Mud-Volcano Controlled Craquelure Paste
- **Ingredients**:
  - 60% Natural Lokbatan Mud-Volcano Clay
  - 25% Acrylic Matte Impasto Paste (e.g., Liquitex Modeling Paste)
  - 15% Water
- **Mechanism**: The acrylic medium provides tensile adhesion to the canvas, while the volcanic clay contracts naturally as water evaporates, producing authentic, dramatic craquelure cracks that will never flake off.

### Recipe D: Bitumen Glaze & Texture Compound
- **Ingredients**:
  - 50% Natural Binagadi Bitumen (dissolved to honey consistency in odorless mineral spirits or linseed oil)
  - 50% Liquitex Gloss Gel Medium or Stand Oil / Dammar Varnish
- **Properties**: Provides a deep, luminous, glass-like petroleum black with golden-brown undertones that dries thoroughly without remaining sticky.

## 3. Step-by-Step Painterly Execution Workflow (Per Canvas)

```
STAGE 1: Heavy Linen Priming & Mineral Ground (Limestone + Coquina Powder)
   │
STAGE 2: Underdrawing & Sgraffito Architecture (Linear Geometry & Form)
   │
STAGE 3: Figurative Execution (Mirgasim's Signature Muse & Caspian Animal)
   │
STAGE 4: Heavy Matter Application (Mud Craquelure, Bitumen Slabs, Real Shells/Pyrite)
   │
STAGE 5: Salt Crystal Embedding & Archival UV Mineral Sealing
```

### Stage 1: Canvas Preparation (Day 1)
1. Mount 450+ gsm Belgian linen onto heavy-duty cross-braced stretcher bars.
2. Apply Recipe A (Absheron Mineral Ground). Work the surface with palette knives to build low stone relief, masonry fissures, and tactile grain. Allow 24 hours to cure.

### Stage 2: Sgraffito & Underdrawing (Day 2)
1. Sketch the statuesque muse and Caspian species using vine charcoal.
2. Using a steel etching needle, dental pick, or pointed palette knife, incise deep **sgraffito lines** through the mineral ground into the underlying canvas, creating the geometric grid lines and architectural framing characteristic of Mirgasim's work.

### Stage 3: Figurative Realization (Days 3–6)
1. Paint the melancholic muse:
   - Sculpt the face with nuanced oil layers: piercing dark eyes, iconic dark connected eyebrow, sculpted cheekbones, and soft melancholic lips.
   - Paint the expressive, elongated hands with careful anatomical precision.
2. Paint the endemic Caspian species in intimate contact with the muse (the seal pup in her arms, the Didacna shell cradled in hand, the levitating shad, or the coiling lamprey).
3. Paint the muse's garments in **modern sculptural/architectural drapes** (plum-burgundy, charcoal bitumen, raw bone linen)—**avoiding carpet patterns or kilim fringes**.

### Stage 4: 3D Geological Matter Embedding (Days 7–9)
1. **For Didacna Shells**: Bed the sterilized shells into pockets of Extra Heavy Gel mixed with coquina dust so they protrude 10–20 mm from the canvas plane.
2. **For Bitumen**: Knife thick, unctuous slabs of Recipe D onto the canvas in bold impasto strokes.
3. **For Mud-Volcano Clay**: Apply Recipe C around the figure and speed-dry with warm air if deeper craquelure fissures are desired.
4. **For Pyrite**: Press brassy pyrite aggregates directly into the wet oil/impasto along the animal's scales or anatomical accents.

### Stage 5: Salt Encrustation & Protective Sealing (Day 10)
1. Trowel Heavy Gloss Gel onto designated limestone ledges and foreground shelves.
2. Press authentic Masazir pink salt crystals into the gel.
3. Allow 48 hours to dry completely clear.
4. Apply 2 fine mist coats of UV-resistant archival matte varnish across the mineral zones to lock the salt crystals in stasis permanently.
